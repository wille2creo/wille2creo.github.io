In August 2015, I transcribed Kahlil Gibran’s “On Love,” from *The Prophet*, in Morse code and made it into a scroll.

Perhaps it was simply the beauty of the language that moved me then. Rereading it these past two days, I find a theology of transcendence I can no longer accept. Love’s meaning must be human, not the meaning of a being beyond us. Transcendent love still seems to me a form of narcissism, bound to the illusion of wholeness and sameness.

And so love is not the narcissism of finding oneself in another, but fidelity to difference—to the Two, rather than the promise of becoming one. It embraces chance and makes a destiny of an encounter. My life is interrupted by you, in your singularity, and through that interruption begins anew, endlessly.

And so love is not merely the lightning of an encounter, but the patient work of building a life: an adventure carried through, creation renewed without end, a victory won again and again over all that stands in its way.

And so love is not a calculation of safety, but a dangerous declaration, made again and again; not a finite pleasure, but the unconditional in a world where everything comes with conditions; not a romantic myth, but communism at its smallest scale, between two people.

And so love will be, has been, and is my truth.
