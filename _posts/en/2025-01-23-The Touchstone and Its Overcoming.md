---
title: The Touchstone and Its Overcoming
locale: en
excerpt: >-
  It is the will that evaluates difficulty—not difficulty that evaluates the will
---

There is a first, undecided response to difficulty: to turn away, see nothing, hear nothing. Reflection can produce the opposite conviction: difficulty must be faced; it is a touchstone by which we prove our worth. Some go further, discovering a test in every circumstance, forever submitting themselves to it. Self-examination becomes a modern religion, inventive above all in its manufacture of guilt.

A more authentic resolve has already taken on the burden of difficulty. Its wisdom lies in knowing that, at some point, both the touchstone and the act of testing gold against it must be overcome, forgotten, transformed.

Only then can values be ranked justly. The value that difficulty attests is derivative and secondary. Ultimately, it is the will that evaluates difficulty—not difficulty that evaluates the will.
