In August 2015, I turned Kahlil Gibran’s “On Love” from The Prophet into a scroll rendered in Morse code.

Perhaps I admired it then simply for the beauty of its language. Rereading it these past two days, I find it steeped in a theology of transcendence — something I now reject. For love’s meaning can only be a human one, not that of a transcendent being; and transcendent love is still a form of narcissism, bound to the illusion of wholeness and identity.

And so love is not a narcissistic attachment to resemblance and identity, but fidelity to difference and to the Two; the embrace and conquest of chance; the interweaving and coupling of affinity and destiny; my life, interrupted for a moment by the singularity that is you, beginning anew, eternally.

And so love is not merely the lightning flash of an encounter, but the sustained work of construction, an adventure pursued to the end, ceaseless re-creation, a victory that forever overcomes every obstacle.

And so love is not a calculus of security, but a dangerous declaration made again and again; not finite enjoyment, but the unconditional in a world pervaded by conditions; not mythic romance, but communism in its smallest unit, between two people.

And so love will be, has been, and is my truth.
